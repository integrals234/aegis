## 1. What M3 was for

M0 built the platform, M1 the exchange core, M2 the futures data stack and the
deterministic replay engine. M2 could *declare* a delayed, missing, duplicated
or gapped market-data record but could not *respond* to one, because responding
requires a participant — and no participant existed.

M3 builds that participant: the market-data vocabulary and publisher on the
exchange side, and on the participant side a feed handler, book reconstruction,
microstructure features, a generic online-statistics library, an order
management system with a mandatory risk seam, portfolio accounting, and
process-boundary state recovery.

It is the first milestone activated under R8 protected-main governance, and the
first to populate seven architecture layers at once.

## 3. Architecture delivered

Seven layers populated, under the four-change `m3-architecture-transition`
approval granted in PR #7 and no others:

1. **add `cpp-participant-app`** — the participant composition root, mirroring
   `cpp-exchange-app`. Nothing else may legally see feed handler, book builder,
   statistics, OMS and portfolio at once. It is the only participant layer
   permitted an edge to `cpp-replay`, which is how M2's fault injector reaches
   the participant for AEGIS-060/061.
2. **`cpp-bindings += cpp-statistics`** — required by AEGIS-107's
   cross-language comparison and by nothing else.
3. **`cpp-exchange-app += cpp-exchange-market-data`** — wires the market-data
   publisher to the book it observes.
4. **`cpp-statistics.may_depend_on` narrowed** from
   `[cpp-common, cpp-participant-book-builder]` to `[cpp-common]` — the
   statistics library is generic numeric code that knows nothing of books,
   orders, feeds or participants, so M4–M7 can reuse it without inheriting
   participant-domain types.

One consequential edit accompanies delta 1: `cpp-participant-app` is added to
`banned.mutable_globals_forbidden_in`. That is a **tightening** — the new layer
becomes subject to the existing ban — not a weakening. No `may_depend_on` set
is widened beyond deltas 2 and 3, no layer is re-dated, and no banned-construct
list is relaxed.

**No production participant→exchange edge exists**, in either direction.
`cpp-participant-oms` depends only on `[cpp-common, cpp-events,
cpp-participant-risk]`; `cpp-participant-app` names no `cpp-exchange-*` layer.
The exchange and the participant meet only through versioned messages — at a
process/stream boundary, or in a test-only integration harness under `tests/`,
which `covered_roots` deliberately excludes.

Five ADRs record the load-bearing decisions: **0020** (participant layering,
composition root, market-data wire vocabulary), **0021** (book reconstruction,
sequence validation, staleness, gap recovery), **0022** (online-statistics
conventions and the Python reference boundary), **0023** (OMS lifecycle, the
mandatory risk seam, the adapter/transport boundary), **0024** (participant
state snapshot and recovery).

## 4. Market data and reconstruction

Market-data messages take permanent numbers in the exchange band of
`cpp/events`, published by an **observing** `cpp/exchange/market_data` that is
never called from `MatchingEngine` and cannot alter emitted-event order.

`BookBuilder` serves both reconstruction modes from one type: an order-level
map is populated for entries carrying a nonzero `order_id` (MBO, AEGIS-066);
a feed that never supplies one drives the aggregated levels alone via
`kPriceLevelSet` (MBP, AEGIS-067). Sequence validation reports gap, duplicate
and reset diagnostics distinctly. Staleness is detected on **both** a
configurable elapsed-time condition and a consecutive-sequence-fault condition,
on a virtual clock. Gap recovery buffers deltas, re-bases on a fresh snapshot,
and replays exactly the buffered deltas a gap-free feed would still have
delivered.

Microstructure features: top-of-book (spread and mid engaged only when both
sides exist), quantity-weighted microprice with defined edge cases, multi-level
depth imbalance, trade/cancellation intensity (event extraction in the book
builder, the generic rolling-rate estimator in `cpp-statistics`, composed at
the app layer), and queue depletion plus post-fill adverse selection.

## 5. Online statistics

Nine estimators in `cpp/statistics`, all over plain numeric observations:
rolling mean/variance/standard deviation, covariance, correlation, leakage-free
z-score (scored against the *prior* window), exponential mean/variance
(Finch's recursion), realized volatility and beta, and online drawdown with
P&L moments.

Two Python modules sit alongside them, with different jobs — a distinction M3
originally got wrong and the closure audit corrected:

- `python/common/online_stats.py` mirrors the C++ recursion step for step. It
  is an executable specification and a check that the binding layer transports
  values faithfully. It is **not** an independent check of the recursion,
  because it *is* the recursion.
- `python/common/offline_stats.py` is the genuinely independent reference:
  every quantity computed from its textbook definition with deliberately
  different algorithms — two-pass variance and covariance, the EW mean
  expanded as a weighted sum over the whole history rather than as a
  recurrence, drawdown by plain scan. Slower and less numerically careful on
  purpose; obviously correct against the definition, which is what a reference
  needs to be.

**Cross-language result (AEGIS-107):** the compiled C++ agrees with
`offline_stats.py` to a worst-case **5.684e-14** absolute difference against a
stated 1e-9 tolerance, across fifteen series. A small nonzero residual is the
expected signature of two different-but-correct algorithms — an exact 0.0
everywhere is what exposed the earlier transliterated reference. One series,
exponential variance, does report exactly 0.0 because no closed weighted-sum
form matches the convention; the artifact records that as a transliteration
check rather than letting it pass as independent. The committed report is
`experiments/evidence/AEGIS-107/cross_language_validation.json`.

## 6. OMS, execution and accounting

Ten frozen lifecycle states with an explicit legal-transition table; invalid
transitions are **rejected, not ignored**, and the table is exercised over every
ordered pair of states. `RiskPending` is structural: no path reaches `Submitted`
without a `RiskDecision`.

Market, passive-limit and aggressive-limit execution are proven against a
**real `ExchangeNode` running unmodified M1 FIFO matching**, driven through
`TransportExecutionAdapter` over a test-only `ExecutionTransport` composed in
`tests/cpp/unit/test_participant_exchange_integration.cpp`. Multi-level sweeps
with genuine residuals, partial-fill accumulation, and portfolio reconciliation
all run against that real engine.

`RecordedResponseAdapter` supplies cancel/amend race orderings and rejection
shapes no live engine can be made to emit on demand. It is **never the sole
proof of an execution requirement** and proves nothing about matching.

AEGIS-119's environment-independence is proven directly: the same
`OrderManager` call sequence run once through each adapter reaches identical
lifecycle states for equivalently shaped responses.

Latency (AEGIS-113) is modelled as committed deterministic per-stage durations
over the typed clock domains ADR-0002 defined and left unused —
`ReceiveTime → DecisionTime → SubmitTime → AckTime` — bridged deliberately at
exactly one place. Attribution reconciles the total path exactly.

Fees are exact integer parts-per-million of notional; slippage is a signed
per-unit deviation from a caller-supplied reference, sign-normalised by side.
Net-P&L reconciliation computes the same figure two independent ways and
requires them equal. Missed trades accumulate the untraded remainder of
rejected/terminated orders from quantities the OMS already tracks — deliberately
**not** counterfactual profit, which would need a strategy.

Portfolio accounting is average-cost with a documented flip-through-zero rule,
verified by a double-entry property test (`cash + quantity·average_price −
realized_pnl == −fees`) over generated fill sequences, single- and
multi-instrument.

## 7. Recovery

Two mechanisms, one contract, deliberately not conflated:

- **AEGIS-061/070 — in-stream feed recovery.** A gap is detected mid-feed,
  deltas are buffered, a fresh market-data *message* re-bases the book, buffered
  deltas replay onto it. The artifact is a wire message.
- **AEGIS-237 — process-boundary recovery.** A byte-stable *file* snapshot of
  OMS and portfolio state, restored into a fresh process. The artifact is a file
  produced and consumed by a shutdown/startup sequence.

`ParticipantSnapshot` v1 composes two independently-owned codecs — each module
owns the snapshot of the state it is responsible for, per
`docs/RECOVERY_CONTRACT.md` obligation 1 — behind one outer version, each
component length-prefixed and validated entirely by its owner.

**Process-boundary result:** a run split at step 9 of a committed 15-step
scenario (a partially-filled order, a fully-filled order on a second instrument,
a rejected order and a cancellation all live at the split), snapshotted, and
restored into a fresh process, reproduces the tail of an uninterrupted run
**byte for byte**. Snapshot bytes are identical across repeated captures.
Truncated, corrupt and unknown-version payloads are all refused
deterministically. Book-builder state is deliberately absent from this snapshot.

## 12. Defects found and fixed

**During implementation (slices 1–7):**

1. **`client_order_id` participant-scoping violation** (production bug,
   `OrderManager`). `handle_order_accepted`/`handle_order_rejected` matched on
   `client_order_id` alone, but ADR-0011 scopes that id to
   `(participant_id, client_order_id)`. A second participant reusing the same
   id silently clobbered this manager's tracked order, double-applying a fill.
   Found only because the real-exchange integration harness used two
   participants with colliding ids. Both handlers now require `participant_id`
   to agree.
2. **Missing `kCancelPending → kAcknowledged` transition.** The Slice 1 table
   had no legal path back to a live state after a rejected cancel — the fourth
   cancel-race outcome. Added.
3. **`TrackedOrder` had no `price_units`.** Genuine order state, absent until
   the snapshot codec needed a complete record.
4. Three test-fixture defects in the integration harness (an off-lot quantity,
   a wrong terminal-state expectation, and five event literals omitting
   `participant_id`), plus one missing guard in a test-local offline helper.

**Found by the independent audit at closure, and fixed:**

5. **AEGIS-107's Python "independent reference" was a transliteration.**
   `python/common/online_stats.py` mirrored the C++ reverse-Welford recursion
   line for line — identical variable names, branch structure and operation
   order. That is why every reported divergence was exactly `0.0`: a
   transliteration agreeing with its source proves nothing about the source.
   The independence claim appeared in the module docstring, in ADR-0022 and in
   the committed evidence artifact. **Fixed** by writing
   `python/common/offline_stats.py` — every quantity computed from its textbook
   definition with deliberately different algorithms (two-pass variance and
   covariance, the EW mean expanded as a weighted sum rather than a recurrence,
   drawdown by plain scan) — repointing the evidence at it, and retracting the
   claim in all three places. The divergence is now genuinely nonzero
   (worst 5.684e-14), which is the expected signature of two different-but-
   correct algorithms. One series, EW variance, still reports exactly `0.0`
   because no closed weighted-sum form matches the convention; the artifact
   says so explicitly rather than letting it pass as independent.
6. **AEGIS-113 modelled three of the five stages its frozen description
   names**, was never called from production, and its `reconciles()` summed
   consecutive differences — an identity that could not fail. **Fixed:** all
   five stages (feed, decision, gateway, exchange, acknowledgement) carried
   through their own clock domains, wired into `OrderManager::submit_new_order`,
   and reconciliation now checked against an independently supplied
   acknowledgement stamp, with a case asserting that removing a stage changes
   the total.
7. **AEGIS-116's fee and slippage model had zero callers.** **Fixed:** fees and
   signed slippage now accrue on every fill inside `OrderManager`. A second
   audit pass found the reconciliation still vacuous on the fee leg — the fee
   cancelled algebraically on both sides and cash was never asserted — and
   found the app layer running two independent fee ledgers. Both fixed: the fee
   is now reconciled by difference against a fee-free run, cash is asserted
   outright, and one `FeeSchedule` is shared.
8. **AEGIS-117's missed-trade tracker had zero callers**, and opportunity cost
   (named in the frozen description) was not computed. **Fixed:** missed trades
   record on both terminal paths, and opportunity cost is measured against a
   caller-supplied mark — the OMS observes no market and will not invent one.
9. **clang-tidy's first full-policy run over M3 found 124 violations.** No
   checkpoint ran tree-wide `clang-tidy`, so this was accumulated debt the
   build-first cadence deferred to closure. All were fixed mechanically — no
   check disabled, no `WarningsAsErrors` weakened, no blanket `NOLINT`, no
   assertion weakened. The largest group (47 `bugprone-unchecked-optional-access`)
   was fixed by converting `*opt` to `opt.value()`, which turns undefined
   behaviour into a defined throw while preserving every assertion.

**Also found at Checkpoint 2:**

10. **AEGIS-107's evidence artifact was unregistered** in
   `implementation_status.json` — an uncited artifact is re-checked by nothing.
   Registered. (Found at Checkpoint 2.)
11. **The compiled bindings' public-surface allowlist was stale.** Slice 4 added
   seven pure `*_batch` functions without updating the exact-set assertion in
   `tests/integration/test_bindings_roundtrip.py`. Extended; ADR-0005 is
   unaffected since the additions are pure functions over plain numerics.
   (Found at Checkpoint 2.)
12. **AEGIS-107's artifact carried no `producer` field**, unlike every other
   artifact. Added.

## 13. Known limitations and carried debt

Stated plainly, because none of it is hidden by the evidence above:

- **All M3 scenarios are synthetic and committed in-repository.** No real venue
  data, no live feed, no real counterparty. Every fixture is hand-built.
- **No paper or live gateway.** M3 ships a transport *seam* and no transport:
  no socket, no broker protocol, no credential, no session or reconnect logic,
  no external account reconciliation. That is AEGIS-221/222 at M9.
- **No risk engine.** M3 delivers the mandatory `RiskGate` seam and ships **no
  production implementation of it**. The only implementations are test doubles,
  explicitly named as such. Position limits, order limits, kill switch and risk
  policy are M5 (AEGIS-120, AEGIS-062, AEGIS-063).
- **No strategy logic.** M4 owns that; no trading return is computed or claimed
  anywhere in M3.
- **Queue position is an approximation**, labelled as one in its own header. It
  assumes a caller-supplied cancellation rate and treats volume ahead as
  uniformly consumable. It observes no per-order queue truth, because a
  participant consuming a public feed has none. Its validation is entirely
  synthetic.
- **No performance claim.** AEGIS-113 models committed deterministic durations;
  it measures nothing. The statistics artifacts record no timing. Nothing in M3
  is a latency, throughput, HFT or comparative performance claim
  (`docs/BENCHMARK_POLICY.md`, `docs/CV_CLAIMS_POLICY.md`, both frozen).
- **M1's historical benchmark artifacts remain captured from a dirty worktree
  at `b33ca67`** (`experiments/evidence/AEGIS-036/`, `AEGIS-039/`). They are
  deliberately **not** rewritten: rewriting historical evidence to look tidier
  than the run that produced it is exactly the dishonesty the provenance stamp
  exists to prevent.
- **Portfolio average-price uses integer division** when adding to an open
  position in the same direction, compounding truncation across repeated adds.
  This is real and documented; the conservation property test states it rather
  than hiding it behind a tolerance.
- **AEGIS-107's latency and memory comparison is owed at M8**, registered as an
  owner-approved residual with an append-only ledger entry. The numerical
  output/error half is complete and evidenced.
- **Later residuals remain due at their approved milestones** and are untouched
  by M3: AEGIS-004→M4, AEGIS-024→M4, AEGIS-059→M9, AEGIS-062→M5,
  AEGIS-063→M5, AEGIS-238→M5.
