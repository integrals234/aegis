## 1. What M2 was for

M1 delivered a deterministic exchange core. M2 delivers the two things a
research platform needs before any strategy can exist: a **futures data stack**
that turns raw multi-market files into one normalized, quality-checked,
queryable, roll-adjusted series, and a **deterministic replay core** that can
drive that data through a virtual clock at four pacing modes with reproducible
fault injection.

The organising constraint is stated once in `cpp/replay/replay_event.hpp` and
enforced everywhere after: deterministic replay is only possible if the input
has **exactly one legal order**. That order is
`(event_time_ns, source_sequence, contract_symbol, record_index)`, and
`record_index` — assigned once by ingestion, persisted, and never recomputed —
is what makes it total rather than partial.

## 4. Architecture delivered

**`python/futures`** — `identifiers`, `instruments`, `contracts`, `chain`,
`calendars`, `schema`, `ingest`, `quality`, `columnar`, `roll/` (four policies),
`series`, `roll_audit`, `roll_sensitivity`, `replay`.

**`cpp/replay`** — `replay_event` (the canonical order), `replay_stream`
(load + validate, fail-closed), `virtual_clock` (structurally incapable of
reading the system clock), `replay_manifest` (FNV-1a reproducibility digest),
`replay_engine` (cursor/resume), `pacing` (four modes), `fault_injection`
(eleven kinds), and `replay_run_main` (the CLI).

`cpp-replay` has **no dependency edge to any `cpp-exchange-*` layer**, in either
direction; the two compose at the process boundary only. `cpp-bindings` gained
exactly one new edge, to `cpp-replay`, and no other.

Decisions are recorded in ADR-0015 (contract identity), ADR-0016 (schema,
ingestion, interchange), ADR-0017 (roll policies and adjustments), ADR-0018
(replay core, pacing, feed boundary) and ADR-0019 (deterministic fault
injection).

## 5. Deterministic replay proof

Determinism is shown the way this repository already requires for AEGIS-005 —
**across separate OS processes**, not across two calls in one binary:

- `aegis_replay_run` produces byte-identical stdout across independent
  invocations with different `PYTHONHASHSEED`
  (`tools/determinism_check.py --producer futures_replay --runs 3`).
- A run resumed from a cursor **in a fresh process** reproduces exactly the tail
  of an uninterrupted run.
- All four pacing modes emit the identical canonical `record_index` sequence;
  only the computed wait differs.
- Fault injection is explicit committed data, never sampled: emitted records
  plus accounted-for dropped records reconstruct the input set exactly.
- `VirtualClock` never sleeps and never reads the system clock, so no figure in
  this milestone is a timing measurement.

The committed stream replayed is derived from the committed bar samples by the
real ingestion pipeline; `tests/replay/test_futures_replay_determinism.py`
fails if it ever drifts from what that pipeline produces.

## 8. Defects found and fixed during closure

Closure verification found seven defects in the accepted slice-13 tree. All were
fixed; none was waived.

1. **Five owner-approved residuals were never registered.** AEGIS-024, 060, 061,
   062 and 063 were `implemented` with no obligation, so the auditor would have
   permitted promoting them to `verified` — a claim their acceptance criteria do
   not support. All five are now on the deferred register.
2. **`tools/update_status.py` authored catalogue entries the auditor rejects.**
   `--blocked-until` wrote the live field without the `deferral_history` ledger
   that must accompany it. Root cause of (1). Fixed, with five regression tests.
3. **Replay determinism was only ever proven in-process.** Added
   `aegis_replay_run` and a cross-process determinism producer.
4. **AEGIS-056's "metrics" did not exist.** Added rate-control metrics measured
   in scheduled virtual time.
5. **AEGIS-057's "interactive" did not exist.** Added `--interactive`, stepping
   one timestamp group per command.
6. **AEGIS-060's delay was declared but never realized.** The delay now
   lengthens the record's scheduled wait rather than moving it.
7. **`docs/TRACEABILITY_MATRIX.md` and `docs/DEFERRED_VERIFICATION.md` were
   stale**, and AEGIS-011/012 evidence still pointed at a slice-2 commit.

Two further corrections: evidence `dirty` could not answer the question it
exists for during batch regeneration (fixed by
`tools/evidence_provenance.py`), and the AEGIS-013 note misstated which code
paths import `zoneinfo`.

## 9. Known limitations and carried debt

- **Every figure in this milestone is synthetic.** All three product families,
  every price, volume and open-interest value are generated fixtures. No claim
  is made about any real market (`docs/DATA_AND_RESEARCH_POLICY.md`).
- **No performance claim is made.** The replay core never sleeps and never reads
  a wall clock; its "rate" metrics are scheduling arithmetic over virtual time,
  not throughput measurements. `docs/BENCHMARK_POLICY.md` figures remain M1's
  exchange benchmarks only.
- **The six residuals above** are the milestone's real carried debt.
- **M1 benchmark provenance.** The M1 benchmark artefacts were captured from a
  dirty worktree at `b33ca67`; that historical record is deliberately left
  as-is rather than rewritten, and remains disclosed in the M1 report.
- Fault injection is a **mechanism**, not a simulation: `ReplayEvent` carries
  identity and ordering only, so a "spread widening" is a labelled record, not a
  computed spread. The header and ADR-0019 say so plainly.
